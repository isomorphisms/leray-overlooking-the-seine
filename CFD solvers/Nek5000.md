# Nek5000 — curved high-order spectral elements

**Source:** [Nek5000](https://github.com/Nek5000/Nek5000) · [spectral-element theory](https://nek5000.github.io/NekDoc/theory.html) · [related modern implementation nekRS](https://nekrs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)

**Numerics.** Primarily Fortran/C, solving incompressible Navier–Stokes with high-order spectral elements on curved, conforming quadrilateral/hexahedral geometry. High-order polynomial approximation can resolve pressure gradients and vortical structures with comparatively low numerical diffusion.

**Why riff on it.** A fundamentally different spatial discretization gives a serious cross-check on low-order Cartesian/finite-volume calculations. Detailed pressure and wall-stress accuracy matters when the alleged lift gain is partly a small local suction difference.

**Concrete first experiment.** Construct a *body-conforming* mesh for a 3D isolated denticle on a short flat-plate patch. Compare streamwise vorticity, separated zone length, and integrated pressure/viscous forces with Basilisk or Xcompact3d. Use systematic element-size and polynomial-order refinement.

**Risk.** Sharp denticle tips and closely spaced ridges are a meshing challenge: smooth high-order elements may have poor Jacobians around the shapes. Resolution in the fluid boundary layer cannot be supplied merely by increasing polynomial order far away. Full airfoil DNS may demand large parallel resources.

**Borrow:** spectral-element weak forms, curved-element representation, pressure preconditioning, high-order verification.

**Decision:** independent numerical-accuracy reference; do not choose it first for large geometry-parameter sweeps.
