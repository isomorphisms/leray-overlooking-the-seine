# J. Scott Turner — Physics of Life

J. Scott Turner's **Physics of Life** teaches physics through organisms, experiments and visible physical effects. Turner taught at the **SUNY College of Environmental Science and Forestry in Syracuse**; his [author page](https://jscottturner.com/) identifies the course and describes the development of its instructional videos.

In the **Reynolds-number** lecture, Turner uses a water flume, cylinders and fluorescent dye to make flow patterns visible. He changes cylinder size and flow speed, examines the near-surface flow, and discusses stagnation, separation, recirculation and alternating vortex shedding. Reynolds number provides the connection between those observations and comparisons across object sizes and fluids. This summary follows the transcript; it does not claim an independent viewing or measurement of the demonstration.

## Four-part course catalogue

Turner's structured course pages supply an index to the wider series. These Udemy catalogues include previews and enrollment; the YouTube links above remain the direct video references.

| Part | Author's course page | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Thermodynamics](https://www.udemy.com/course/physics-of-life-1-thermodynamics/) | Energy, metabolism, open systems and biological disequilibrium. |
| 2 | [Biomechanics](https://www.udemy.com/course/physics-of-life-2-biomechanics/) | Material properties, skeletons, elastic energy and locomotion. |
| 3 | [Fluids](https://www.udemy.com/course/physics-of-life-3-fluids/) | Viscosity, boundary layers, Reynolds number, drag, swimming and flight. |
| 4 | [Wave phenomena](https://www.udemy.com/course/physics-of-life-4-wave-phenomena/) | Light, electromagnetism, sound, sensing and communication. |

The **Fluids** catalogue connects the physical topics to sponge feeding, caddisfly cases, burrow ventilation, microbial swimming and animal flight. Its sections on boundary layers, drag, vortices and wings are particularly relevant to this collection.

## Connection to the Leray and CFD reading

**Editorial connection:** these demonstrations give physical examples for the vocabulary in the [CFD solver notes](../CFD%20solvers/README.md) and the [theory, experiments and flow-control notes](theory-experiment-and-flow-control.md). The cylinder experiment offers a concrete reference for thinking about separation and wakes. Biological examples make the consequences of changing size and shape easier to inspect.

The [shark-denticle lift investigation](../CFD%20solvers/shark-denticle-lift.md) raises related questions about near-wall momentum, pressure and vortices. Those connections motivate comparison; the videos do not establish the denticle mechanism or validate a solver.
