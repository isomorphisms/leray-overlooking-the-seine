# Stephanie Frank Singer: symplectic geometry through an orbit

## Symmetry in Mechanics: A Gentle, Modern Introduction

**Stephanie Frank Singer (2001).** Birkhäuser. [Publisher, description, and contents](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-0189-2) · [Author's page](http://symmetrysinger.com/index.php?id=math-physics).

Singer builds the book around the two-body problem. It first derives planetary motion using familiar mechanics, then revisits the same problem using symplectic geometry. The abstractions acquire a concrete task: explain how motion, symmetry, and conserved quantities fit together.

A mechanical state records positions and momenta together. A symplectic form supplies the pairing that turns an energy function into a direction of motion. Symmetry connects infinitesimal motions with conserved quantities; a momentum map packages those quantities. Under suitable hypotheses, reduction fixes conserved values and identifies states related by the remaining symmetry.

## Questions for the fluid thread

What plays the role of phase space when a state describes an entire fluid? Which apparent freedoms come from changing the labels assigned to fluid particles? What geometric structure survives reduction?

These are proposed next questions. Singer gives a manageable finite-dimensional example; [Arnold–Khesin and Marsden–Ratiu](foundations.md) take the discussion toward continuum mechanics. The symplectic geometry is also worth pursuing before choosing any numerical method.
